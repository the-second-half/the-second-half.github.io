October was mostly the recovery period after fistula surgery. The diet was restricted, and I could not run or walk long distances. Detailed daily records of weight and food only start on 22 October — from 1 to 21 October there is no daily food data, so those days are left out.

### 2. Food and supplements in October

Going through the food logs for the whole window (10.22–10.31), I grouped what I actually ate and the supplements I took. The food base is healthy, precise and varied — and there were a few very honest "off the rails" days.

#### 2.1 Main protein

(1) Eggs: mostly selenium-enriched eggs, one or two a day, occasionally one raw and one cooked — the most stable base of my breakfast.

(2) Chicken: skinless chicken fillet, three or four pieces at a time, about 120–180 g, very frequent.

(3) Red meat: pork tenderloin, once as much as 300 g; a little pork liver in early November.

(4) Fish: Norwegian Atlantic salmon trim, belly and chest, 250 g a time, two or three meals a week.

(5) Soy: firm tofu from northern Jiangsu, very frequent, 200–400 g a time.

(6) Dairy: German skimmed milk, 200–250 ml, usually as a snack or at dinner.

(7) Meat eaten out: braised chicken, shredded pork with water bamboo, stinky mandarin fish, beef from soup.

#### 2.2 Main carbohydrates

(1) Oats: Pangdonglai quick-cook wholegrain oats, 30 g normally, sometimes more.

(2) Tubers: steamed or boiled potato, very frequent, about 150 g; Lipu taro, frequent, 150 g; sweet potato.

(3) Carbohydrates eaten out: sesame flatbread, white rice (once 150 g, three liang, in one meal), pickled-vegetable pork mooncakes (four in one day on 30 October).

#### 2.3 Vegetables and mushrooms

(1) High-fibre, high-potassium vegetables: celery (once 600–900 g in a single sitting), tomato (very frequent), okra, bok choy, leaf lettuce, baby bok choy, lettuce, cabbage.

(2) Mushrooms: white button mushroom (often 300–500 g), king oyster mushroom, shimeji.

(3) Roots: carrot, boiled or eaten raw.

#### 2.4 Fruit and nuts

(1) Fruit: red pomelo (very frequent — several times a whole 800 g fruit with the peel), kiwi, apple.

(2) Nuts and seeds: raw sweet almonds (5–10 at a time), roasted pumpkin seeds (15–20 at a time).

#### 2.5 Supplements and special drinks

(1) Zinc gluconate oral liquid: after the adjustment on 27 October, fixed at 20 ml every morning, about 10 mg elemental zinc.

(2) Multivitamin: one tablet after breakfast every day.

(3) Vitamin D: one capsule a day, with food.

(4) Selenium yeast: one tablet a day (100 mcg) at first, stopped on 29 October and replaced entirely by selenium-enriched eggs.

(5) Lactulose oral solution, imported from the Netherlands: 30 ml a day at first, then adjusted between 10 and 15 ml according to stool, later reduced or stopped.

#### 2.6 Olive oil

For dressing boiled vegetables, one tablespoon at a time.

### 3. What the numbers say

(1) Weight fell off a cliff: from 78.55 kg on 23 October down to 74.80 kg on 31 October, steady the whole way. At the end of the month it broke through the 75 kg mark.

(2) Food behaved like a roller coaster:

· High points (10.26 / 10.28 / 10.29): fat, carbohydrate and protein in near-perfect balance, calories steady around 1400–1500.

· Danger point (10.27): protein as high as 150 g and fibre as high as 35 g — an extreme load on the kidneys and the gut.

· Blow-out (10.30): four mooncakes pushed calories past 2000 and fat close to 90 g; the low-calorie day before and a strong metabolic base carried it.

(3) Fibre swung between two extremes: never stable — about 10 g on 10.24, which left stool in a lump, and 35 g on 10.27, which made the gut produce gas wildly. That is the core reason stool was sometimes dry, sometimes loose.

(4) In summary: the food was clean, mostly boiled or steamed; salmon, chicken breast, firm tofu, oats, tomato and mushrooms formed the frame. But there were extremes as well — vegetable portions too large, fibre often over the top, pomelo regularly overdone, and the four-mooncake ambush on 30 October. All through October, luckily, the body kept a strong metabolism and kept stool moving through all that pulling.

### 4. The goal for November

Let the four lines — calories, protein, fat, carbohydrate — run flatter. No more roller coaster.
