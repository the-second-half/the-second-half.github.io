My story is the story of tens of millions of Chinese men born in the 1970s.

People call me Lao Xia, or Mr. Xia when they are being polite. Male, born in 1976, a northerner, the real thing. Like most of my generation I caught the best years: born at the start of reform and opening up, then forty years of growth faster than anything the country had seen before. The country's luck was my luck. I followed my parents out of a northern village and put down roots in one of the big second-tier cities. On top of what they had built, I went to school, got into university, found a job, married, had a child. Then I got lucky in a second way: I entered real estate and climbed. Site engineer, department manager, deputy general manager of a local company, general manager, and finally one of the senior managers of a property group. On paper, in background and career, I did better than most people who work for a living. I am grateful to the thirty good years of Chinese real estate, to my parents, and to my own grinding. I was proud. I thought the road would run straight to sixty, and that even at seventy I would still be unwilling to sit still.

Everything has a price. To climb faster and earn more, I gave up family, health, hobbies — all of it went into the work. I lived in other cities for years, apart from my family. There were dinners and receptions. There were nights I drank until they put a drip in my arm. I almost never left the office on time. Staying up late was normal. The pressure never eased: sales targets, opening dates, completion inspections, financing deadlines. I knew it was bad for me. I knew I was missing my parents, my wife, my child. But I kept telling myself it would be fine later. That went on for thirty years.

Some of it may be in the family. I was fat from childhood. Elementary school, middle school, university, and then work — I tried to lose it several times and failed every time.

Born fat, plus thirty years running hot, left me with a body that was never really well.

(1) Overweight. I am 178 cm. At my heaviest I was 105 kg, and for years I sat between 95 and 100.
(2) High blood pressure. I was already hypertensive at my first check-up after starting work, and still am. It moves around 150/100.
(3) High blood lipids, and fatty liver — severe, inevitably. My liver enzymes were never normal.
(4) Hashimoto's, I don't know exactly when. Around thirty-five, I think. Three of the five thyroid markers were off, and it was drifting toward hypothyroidism.
(5) Thyroid nodules at some point. Kidney stones too, at some point. Neither gave me symptoms.
(6) Blood sugar, hovering near the line for years.
(7) Bad insomnia. Nerves worn thin.
(8) Knees worn out. In winter there is a cold ache inside them.
(9) A short temper. Anxiety.

I knew this would end badly. I just kept thinking I was still young, that retirement would fix it, and that meanwhile there was money to be made. I know plenty of people like this — every industry, every age. Ordinary people, the same instincts, the same choices.

Then one day everything changed.

Chinese real estate began to slide in 2021. Nobody expected it to slide for five years and still be sliding. Nobody knows how long it will last or how deep it goes. Maybe we get our own lost thirty years, and a whole generation gets buried in the rubble. I don't know. What I know is that along with the industry, my job, my income and my investments all broke at once.

(1) When property was booming I put every yuan of my savings into high-yield property trust products. I even picked a company I thought was solid. It defaulted in 2021, and a lifetime of savings turned into money I could not get back.
(2) The company I worked for stopped. Sales froze, assets lost value, loans went past due, construction halted. Income stopped, and stayed stopped. Overnight I had lost my savings and my income. I stopped sleeping. The anxiety was constant. I could not see a way forward.

Long damage to the body, long years of pressure, a heavy financial loss: three knives, cutting into my life one after another. I felt myself coming apart. The last straw was two check-ups, in May and November of 2023. Fasting glucose above 7 both times, rising, peaking at 7.27. That broke the last of my self-deception. I understood that I stood at the edge of a total collapse, one small step from going under, with death already waving at me.

The moment I held that report, I woke up. There was no way back. Life itself had reached a line. Fear made me decide to change. Fear made me drop the props and look at what my life actually was. Fear made me willing to pay any price to rebuild the second half.

That was November 2023. I was 47.
