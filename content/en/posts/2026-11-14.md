![Six rules on one page](/images/selfcontrol-six-en.png)

Losing weight is a fight against your own bad habits. The craving for good food, the reluctance to move, the urge to drink when you are low — these are normal psychological and physiological reactions. Life also puts you in situations you cannot avoid: drinking at a business dinner, weeks of indoor rest while ill, a particular moment when you need food to fight your mood. All of it is unavoidable. These weaknesses, and these abnormal moments, need to be understood correctly, and need a plan.

### 1. Weakness and self-control are both part of a normal character

Neither indulge the weakness, nor fight it for years on end. Go with human nature, and let good habits grow gradually.

### 2. Health is your own business — don't rely on outsiders to supervise

If your own understanding hasn't got there, if you don't have a strong and firm desire to lose weight, then telling friends, colleagues and family your plan and asking them to supervise you won't work. It won't last, and it only adds to your mental load. Do not join weight-loss groups, running groups or gym groups, and don't look for a weight-loss buddy. Most of these organisations exist for commercial or other purposes.

Share and talk anonymously online instead. There's no mental burden that way.

### 3. Never test human nature, never challenge your self-control

The failure rate is high, and even if you hold out, you pay for it in mental torment. What I suggest:

(1) Keep no food at home that is outside the plan — fried pastries, sweets and the like. When you want it and it isn't within reach, you forget about it.

(2) Go to the supermarket after you've eaten. It lowers the urge to buy. Never shop hungry — you'll come home with plenty you regret.

(3) When you really want something, buy the healthiest and most suitable option, even if it costs the most. Better than a pile of junk food. Training your palate is not a bad thing for healthy weight loss.

(4) Some lines cannot be crossed: drinking when it isn't required by work, eating iodine-rich food, and so on. Going with human nature and understanding its weaknesses is not an excuse for indulgence.

(5) If you do give in occasionally, arrange the remedy at once. After a Chongqing hotpot — a lot of oil, meat and offal — drink plenty of water afterwards and eat lightly at the next meal, to speed the junk food out of your body.

### 4. After an occasional slip, don't overreact

"Overreacting" means a remedy that does real damage to the body: fasting for a whole day after a big meal, or suddenly running 20 kilometres. These may cause serious injury and do little to make up for the error.

### 5. Allow for error — don't write yourself off over a slip

Worse than overreacting is denying yourself because of one slip: losing heart, giving up on yourself, abandoning the plan. (1) If you slip often, the plan itself has a problem — it is too hard to execute, it did not go step by step within your capacity — and the plan needs adjusting. (2) If one occasional slip makes you quit entirely, your understanding and motivation are not yet sufficient, and the plan needs adjusting too.

### 6. The right way

Recognise that occasional slips are unavoidable. There are two right moves:

(1) Analyse the cause: is it psychological or physiological? Simply a bad mood, or a nutrient gap created by the recent diet, with the body raising an alarm.

(2) Return to the plan quickly. After a simple remedy, forget the slip and come straight back to the normal plan. Neither overreact nor give up on yourself — just carry on with the original plan. Over a longer cycle, one slip or a few slips does not change the result.

![After a slip, four steps](/images/selfcontrol-reset-en.png)

In short, health management is a very long cycle — a lifetime plan. Within it, "breaking the rules" is normal. First, keep executing: don't let a chance event interrupt you for long. Second, keep revising the plan, lower the difficulty of execution, and avoid relying on willpower for years to fight human nature.
