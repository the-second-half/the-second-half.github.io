On the morning of October 30 the scale stopped at 74.80.

Half a kilo lower than I expected. The night before I was 76.10, and the usual gap puts the morning around 75.2. Off by 0.4. I stepped on again. Still 74.80.

The goal I set for phase one was to break 75 kilos. That day it happened. But I didn't feel like I'd won. I was looking at other things: waist 82, weight 74.8, markers mostly normal — and hair falling out, gallbladder wall thickened, constipation coming back.

My body was saying one thing: this job is done, time for a different job.

## 1. Closing the books on phase one

First, what got done.

Weight: 95.5 kilos at the peak in June 2025, 86.35 when daily records start on 25 May 2026, 74.80 on 30 October. From the peak that's 20.7 kilos, 21.7 percent. From where records begin, 11.75 kilos in five months.

Waist: 100 cm at the peak, 82.0 on 30 October. Down 18 cm. Reliable continuous measurement only starts at 84.5 on 15 August; the last two and a half months took off 2.5 cm, much slower than weight.

Blood lipids: triglycerides 4.56 in October 2025, 2.32 in April 2026, 0.83 in June — inside the reference range. Total cholesterol 5.75 → 6.72 → 5.19. LDL 3.12 → 3.96 → 2.94. All three turned at once.

Thyroid: TSH 10.74 at the peak, then 7.24, then 4.96 — the lowest in two years, still above the 4.2 upper limit.

Fatty liver: moderate → mild-moderate → mild.

Surgery: fistula repair on 23 September. High-fibre diet, warm water rinsing, cold air drying. The wound scabbed clean.

Now what didn't get done.

The antibodies did not move. Thyroglobulin antibody stays above 4000. Peroxidase antibody sits between 179 and 189. Losing weight does not stop an autoimmune attack. I wrote this out in Chapter Eight; here I'll only keep the conclusion — that line is managed with monitoring and medication, not with body weight.

And there was a price. Visible hair loss from 15 August, a long strip about a third of a palm wide on one side of the front. A gallbladder inflammation on the 18 June scan, wall 4.3 mm, the thickest in years. Constipation recurring since May, ten days with entries.

## 2. Why I stopped here

You could ask: if the weight can still come down, why stop.

Because three signals showed up at the same time, and they point at one cause: the pace was too fast, and the bill was starting to arrive.

The first is hair. Mid-August to early September is exactly the window where weight fell fastest and the calorie gap was widest — and where protein and fibre are easiest to run short on.

The second is the gallbladder. Very low fat plus rapid loss means bile stasis and poor emptying. That is where the wall thickness comes from.

The third is constipation. Not enough fibre, not enough water, not enough fat, and the gut stops moving.

None of these is a disease. All three are the body raising a hand. It didn't say stop losing. It said slow down.

Deeper than that: the goal of phase one was never "thin". It was to clear the metabolic channel. The channel is clear — lipids in range, liver enzymes clean, blood sugar stable, TSH at a two-year low. That goal is finished. Pushing weight lower from here has shrinking returns and rising costs. That is the reason to stop.

Weight is an outcome, not a goal. Treat the outcome as the goal and you keep adding weight to the bar until something breaks.

## 3. Four rules for phase two

Phase two runs from October 2026 to October 2027 — one year. In one sentence: stop chasing the scale, hold the band, keep the markers steady, repair the body, prevent relapse.

Same four rules, different content. Phase one was subtraction. This phase is addition.

Eat clean: less, lightly processed, balanced. The emphasis now is enough protein, not less food.

Drink clean: enough, filtered, simple. 2000 to 2500 ml a day, for metabolism and to keep stones away.

Supplement clean: few, on the low side, safe brands. Trimmed to zinc, vitamin D, selenium yeast, magnesium. No stacking.

Sleep well: enough, on time, relaxed. The insomnia that came with low carbs gets solved with moderate carbs, not pills.

## 4. Seven targets

One: weight and waist — fasting 74 to 75 kilos, bedtime 75 to 76; waist 80 to 82 cm.

Two: hold the markers, keep lowering lipids — all panels normal, thyroid improving, LDL trending down.

Three: control the thyroid — nodule (category 4a) and function together, recheck every 6 to 12 months.

Four: hair — hold what I have first, talk about regrowth later.

Five: movement and joints — jogging and walking as the base, add strength work, protect the knees.

Six: keep the fistula from coming back — prevent both constipation and diarrhoea, no shortcuts on care.

Seven: mood and sleep — steady mood, enough sleep.

## 5. What counts as done

### Two weight anchors

Set on 30 October: fasting 74.0 to 75.0, bedtime 75.0 to 76.0.

Two anchors beat one. The morning shows metabolism; the evening shows what you ate. A gap of 1.5 to 2.5 kilos between them is normal. Outside that, adjust the next day.

The method: use last night's bedtime number and this morning's fasting number to adjust today's carbs, protein and total. Don't panic when it rises, don't celebrate when it falls. Watch the seven-day average.

![Weight and the phase-two target band](/images/health-2026-target.png)

### Nutrition

Protein 90 to 120 g a day. Enough to stop hair loss and heal wounds, not so much that it loads the kidneys.

Carbs 150 to 200 g a day. The base for steady blood sugar, sleep and bowel movement. This was the shortfall in phase one; now it gets paid back.

Fat 40 to 55 g a day. Protects the carotid, lubricates the gut, and gives the gallbladder the stimulation it needs. With a 4.3 mm wall, fat cannot go extremely low.

Fibre 25 to 30 g a day. Prevents constipation, but not more — too much blocks the gut.

Fixed food list, so I don't calculate every day: skimmed milk, plain oats, Atlantic salmon trim, selenium-enriched eggs, northern-style firm tofu.

### Medical markers

LDL heading below 2.6 for long-term vessel protection.

TSH held steady, nodule rechecked every 6 to 12 months, and the long-term effect of antibodies above 4000 kept in view.

Kidney: prevent stone recurrence — more water, less sodium, more potassium, moderate oxalate, no blind calcium supplements.

### Surgery and shape

Perianal: prevent constipation and diarrhoea, warm water wash plus cold air dry, full healing, no recurrence.

Hair: fix the nutrition first — pork liver for iron, pumpkin seeds for zinc — then see dermatology about androgenetic loss.

Movement: no more long continuous walks; protect knees and the perianal area. Build back a month after surgery, then add strength training to keep muscle and hold the metabolic rate.

## 6. How I'll know it worked

Not by one number on the scale. By four things.

A year from now, weight is still between 74 and 76 — not sliding down, not back above 80.

At the recheck, LDL is below 2.6, TSH is not above 4.2, the nodule is unchanged in size.

Hair is no thinner, the fistula has not returned in a year, the gallbladder wall has not thickened further.

Sleep is steady, and mood no longer rises and falls with the scale.

If all four hold, phase two worked. Whatever fails gets fixed on its own, not by starting over.

## 7. Last

Phase one took eight months and finished something hard: 95.5 kilos to 74.8, a page of red numbers turned mostly black.

Phase two is about making it stay.

Subtraction runs on discipline. Addition runs on patience. This year I'm not trying to prove anything. I just want to keep what I already have — the weight, the markers, the hair, the wound.

Holding is harder than losing.

Floating, no shore. I am my own boat.
