I was not lazy in 2024. But the results were poor, and some markers got worse — TSH among them. So where was the problem?

Glucose and LDL cholesterol came down. But triglycerides went from 2.45 to 4.56, and TSH kept climbing to 10.74.

Some of the credit goes to how fast short-form media and AI tools developed in these years, and to the free versions offered by a few large platforms here. Knowledge became much cheaper. Information that used to cost money and time now sits in a phone. From early 2023 I read and asked constantly — philosophy, health, personal finance, psychology — looking for how other people had solved the kind of mess I was in.

Slowly I could name the traps I had walked into.

(1) I did not know myself. The refusal to accept a smaller life. The habit of calling a good era my own ability. The gambler's reflex. Everything started here. I had never worked out who I actually was.

(2) I wanted everything perfect. Every part of life, and always more of it. Even while losing weight I insisted on gaining muscle at the same time. I did not have the resources or the luck that a perfect life requires.

(3) I did not know enough. Not about food, exercise, or how chronic disease actually works. Not about how the economy and real estate move. Not about a way of managing money that suited me. I had read too little for too many years and told myself I already understood.

(4) I had no patience. Everything had to happen fast. I could not sit with long uncertainty.

These are faults of character and of how I see the world. They sat behind every decision I made and every move I took.

Through 2025 I kept adjusting how I lived, went back over what had worked and what had not, and deliberately made my life smaller and slower. After two years of grinding it down, I wrote myself a set of rules.

(1) Three legs hold up a life: health, a steady mind, and cash flow. Cut one and the whole thing falls. Any rescue has to work on all three at once.

(2) Solve problems with a system, not with a single bet. Health is a system of eating, drinking, sleeping, moving. Mind is a system of company, solitude, pleasure, release. Money is a system of income, investment, saving. Fixing one thing and hoping is gambling.

(3) Never rely on willpower for anything long. Willpower is small and unreliable. Any plan that only survives on willpower — extreme dieting, for instance — will almost certainly fail.

(4) Break the task down and rank it. When resources are short, split the big goal into small ones, order them by time and importance, and match what you have to what you can carry. If it still does not get done, the piece is still too big. Split again until each piece is almost effortless. Health, for example: first lose weight and ignore muscle loss, because weight is what moves most of the numbers. Once weight is normal, then build strength. The task becomes simple.

(5) Let time do the work. Nothing good is forced. Put every goal on a very long schedule and move slowly. Time is the largest asset I have.

(6) Subtract. Less socializing, less food, less shopping, less contact with my boss. Fewer things pulling at me, so the little energy I have goes to the few things that matter most right now.

(7) Learn to let go. There is always someone above and someone below. Enough is enough.

(8) Use AI as the advisor I could never afford. Ask it, log with it, check myself against it. I record every meal, count the nutrition, and judge whether it was right. Over months, the log, the lab reports and how my body actually feels tell me what to change. This is the one real advantage of the era.

Two years of trying and accumulating, and I had a working method: rebuild a life on the three legs of health, mind and cash flow. After the Spring Festival in early 2026, I began the next round.
