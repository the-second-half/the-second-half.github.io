A steady mind needs a base. Mine has three corners: a body that works, cash that is enough, and a want that stays under control.

![The steady-mind triangle: health, cash, contentment, with a steady mind at the centre](/images/mindset-triangle-06.png)

## 1. Three corners. Lose one and it tips over.

Health comes first. A man who is in pain every day, who is counting down the end, does not have a steady mind. It is not possible.

Past forty, and especially past fifty, almost nobody is fully healthy. Everyone carries something chronic, or worse. What you cannot carry is an illness that is out of control.

Second, money has to be comfortable enough. Not a lot. Just not tight. Some pocket money, and no worry about the basic cost of living. If you do not know where the next meal comes from, your mood will not be good. If you are in debt up to your neck and the creditors are at the door every day, you will be on edge.

Third, keep the wanting small. If the body is not in serious pain and the money has no debt or only a debt you can carry, and there is cash for everyday spending, then what is left is the wanting.

## 2. Contentment is not lying down

Keeping the wanting small does not mean lying flat, giving up, or not trying. It means: nothing that seriously drains your health. Nothing that risks your life. Nothing that invests far beyond your income. Nothing that puts your freedom at risk.

If you cannot hold it and you take one big gamble, winning may lift your life and your standing. Losing may cost you the rest of your life, or your life itself. That is exactly the mistake I made. At nearly fifty, I had to start over.

## 3. A very steady life rarely produces great things

That is the trade-off. And it is a choice.

When you are young, health and time are in surplus. Working hard is right, and I worked hard. Past forty-five, and especially past fifty, unless your conditions are very good, you start to cut: fewer wants, less social life, fewer investments, fewer things to carry in your head.

You stop attacking. You start defending.

## 4. The three things I do

1. **Lose weight.** Stop the health from getting worse, then let it slowly get better.
2. **Keep the job if I can.** Even if the pay is smaller and unsteady. Save through the safest route there is. No investment that keeps me awake.
3. **Cut hard.** If it is not necessary, do not do it. Fewer gatherings, less presence at work, less of everything that is not needed.

## 5. At work, I am stepping back on purpose

Work I would once have fought for, I now let go on purpose. I back down. It feels like a loss. The boss has shouted at me. There is a real chance of a lower title, a lower salary, or the door. I do it anyway.

It is the rational choice. Everything has a price. What matters is what you choose to keep, and what you choose to give up.

## 6. The rest is time

Much of it, especially the damage, only fades with time.

What we have to do is simple: while it heals, do not add new wounds.
