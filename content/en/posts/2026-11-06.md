Start with food. Filter first, then fix it in place. Out of the thousands of things I could eat, I picked the few that fit me — a fixed menu, fixed amounts, a fixed way of cooking. That is my base. It has a little slack in it, for the dinners I cannot avoid, and for the evenings when I am low or hungry and eat a bit more. The fewer things that can vary, the less willpower it takes. This chapter is everything I worked out: what I eat, why I eat it, what I stay away from, what I supplement, how much water I drink.

## Four rules first

1. **Lose weight, but do not lose nutrition.** If the weight does not come down, the blood sugar, the blood fat and the fatty liver will not come down either. But losing hair and having no strength is another illness.
2. **Work around what I already have.** Hashimoto's, thyroid nodules, kidney stones, fatty liver, a thickened carotid wall, surgery for a fistula. Each one narrows the menu.
3. **Control the amount, but never go hungry for long.** Hunger always comes back. Weight lost by starving comes back with interest.
4. **Take the goal — 20 kg in 12 months — and turn it into a daily number.**

Do the arithmetic. A kilo of fat is about 7700 kcal. Twenty kilos is 154,000 kcal. Spread over 365 days, that is a deficit of about 420 kcal a day. I am 1.78 m; holding my weight takes roughly 2100 to 2200 kcal, so I eat about 1700.

Then one level down: 1450–1550 kcal a day, protein above 90 g (about 1.2 g per kilo), fat under 45 g, the rest carbohydrate, around 150 g.

That is not harsh. The hard part is not breaking it, not one single day. So I took the choice out of the kitchen.

## What I have to work around

Start with weight: 95.5 kg in January 2024.

1. Hashimoto's, with a stretch of hypothyroidism. Thyroglobulin antibodies have stayed above 4000 — the limit of what the lab can detect. Peroxidase antibodies 158–228. TSH came down from 10.74 to 4.09.
2. Thyroid nodules, category 4a. Between 2.6 and 2.9 mm, all along.
3. Kidney stone, left side, 3–4 mm.
4. Severe fatty liver.
5. Carotid wall thickened to 1.2 mm, LDL 3.08. That is the blood vessels talking to me.
6. High blood sugar: HbA1c 6.5.
7. High triglycerides and raised liver enzymes.
8. High blood pressure, 150/100.

These numbers have come up in earlier chapters. Here they are in one place, because the menu is decided by them, not by what I like.

## Against each illness: what I can eat, what I cannot

**Thyroid (Hashimoto's, hypothyroidism, nodules)**

- Iodine: not as much as possible, and not zero. Kelp, nori and seaweed do not go on the table every day. I do not chase iodised-free salt either. Both extremes are wrong.
- Cabbage, broccoli, radish — cooked, normal amounts, no problem. Raw and juiced in quantity is where the worry starts.
- Nodules are not managed by diet. They are managed by check-ups.
- Selenium is the only thing here worth supplementing on its own. More later.

**Kidney stone**

- High-oxalate food: control the amount, do not cut it out. Spinach, beetroot, strong tea, chocolate, large handfuls of nuts. I blanch spinach first.
- Calcium must not be cut. Calcium binds oxalate in the gut, so less gets absorbed. Skipping milk to avoid stones is the most common mistake.
- Less salt. More salt, more calcium in the urine.
- More water. This works better than any restriction.
- No large doses of vitamin C. Those 5000 mg effervescent tablets, never.

**Fatty liver, triglycerides**

- Alcohol: not one drop. The only absolute no on this menu.
- Refined sugar, sweet drinks, juice, honey: skip them.
- Fruit in limited amounts: one kind a day, one portion at a time.

**Blood vessels**

- Less saturated fat: fatty meat, animal fat, processed meat.
- Fish and nuts replace some of the red meat.

## One person, away from home: how I cook

Posted away, alone, and by the time I get home I have nothing left. My four conditions: buy once for the week, no knife work, one pot, on the table in ten minutes. A method that fails any of these, I will not keep up, however good it reads.

So it comes down to three things:

1. **One method: boil.** One small electric pot. I eat out of the pot and wash one bowl less.
2. **One base: tomato.** Cooked down until it falls apart, anything can go in — fish, chicken breast, tofu, mushrooms, greens. I drink the broth too.
3. **Blanch the meat first**, pour the water off, then cook it with the vegetables. That takes off most of the floating fat and the purines, and it is the single most useful thing I did this year.

Cook the staple once, keep it cold, thaw as needed. Cooked potato, cooled and chilled, has more resistant starch — the same 150 g keeps me full longer.

## What I finally settled on

Not a list from a nutrition textbook. These are the things I actually eat, the ones that show up hundreds of times in my own logs. None of them are hard to buy: the wet market, an ordinary supermarket, the shop downstairs. No online orders, no imported-food aisle.

**Protein (mostly at noon):** 1–3 eggs a day, two at breakfast; salmon trim 250 g, two or three times a week; chicken tenderloin 3–4 pieces (40–50 g each); pork loin 250–300 g; firm tofu, one 400 g pack every other day; skimmed milk 200 ml.

**Staples (one meal out of three):** oats 30–80 g at breakfast; potato 150 g; taro 150 g; half a multigrain bun.

**Vegetables (300–500 g a meal):** tomato as the base; celery, lettuce, sweet potato leaves, amaranth, baby bok choy, napa cabbage, in rotation; okra, loofah; white mushroom, king oyster, shiitake; carrot raw.

**Fruit and nuts:** cherry tomatoes, prunes, pomelo, pear — one kind, one portion a day; raw almonds 5–8, pumpkin seeds 10–20.

**Oil:** one tablespoon of olive oil, tossed through; a sip of perilla oil now and then.

The full list, with amounts and how often, is at the end of this page.

## Supplements: only four

I made mistakes here. At first I bought whatever I heard was good for me. What I actually need to take long term is four things.

1. **B-complex**, one tablet after breakfast. The B vitamins and choline are in there.
2. **Vitamin D**, 400 IU with food. A maintenance dose, not a treatment dose. Do not raise it on your own. It needs fat to be absorbed.
3. **Selenium yeast**, 100 µg after breakfast. Either this or selenium-rich eggs, never both — two sources is stacking.
4. **Zinc gluconate**, 20 ml, about 10 mg of zinc, in the morning. Keep it two hours away from milk and oats, or it is wasted.

Three rules against overdoing it:

- **Know the ceiling:** selenium 400 µg, zinc 40 mg. More is not better. Past the ceiling it stops being a supplement and becomes a poison.
- **Never stack the same nutrient.** Selenium yeast plus selenium eggs, a multivitamin plus a single vitamin — both count as stacking.
- **Calcium, iron and zinc compete for the same door.** Take them two hours apart.

Lactulose, 15–30 ml, is for constipation after surgery. Not a supplement. Stop when it is not needed.

## Water

2.5–3 litres a day, counting what is in the food. A glass of warm water first thing.

Three reasons: stones, constipation, and protein — a high-protein diet needs water to process it.

How: on a schedule, not when thirsty. Less after dinner, or I am up twice at night. Filtered and boiled. Less strong tea; the oxalate is high.

## In the end

What I learned in two years: it is not that I held back. It is that I no longer have to.

The fridge holds only those things. The pot only does one thing. When I am hungry, what my hand reaches is something I can eat. Dieting is not finally about discipline. It is about the environment — arrange it so that no discipline is needed, and it finally works.

(My own record. Not medical advice.)
