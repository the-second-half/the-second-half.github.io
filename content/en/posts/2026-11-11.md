Dollar deposit rates are plainly higher than renminbi deposit rates right now. Even for deposits inside China, some banks pay 3% a year — Bank of Jiangsu and Hengfeng Bank, for example.

## 1. Advantages of dollar term deposits in China

(1) A 3% annual rate, higher than renminbi deposits.

(2) You can choose a product covered by deposit insurance, with 500,000 renminbi of coverage per person.

(3) The paperwork is simple, exactly like a renminbi deposit. You can do it on your phone.

(4) A simplified foreign exchange quota of 50,000 dollars per person per year.

## 2. Risks

(1) Exchange rate movement. The renminbi has been appreciating recently, so holding dollars carries currency risk.

(2) Buying and selling dollars involves fees.

(3) Anything above 20,000 dollars requires an application.

## 3. A suggestion for working families

For working families with a reasonably stable income, I would treat dollar term deposits as one part of a long-term household asset allocation. The plan follows.

![Table 2 — The four-point plan](/images/usd-deposit-plan-en.png)

## 4. The plan

(1) Choose a Chinese bank with relatively high dollar deposit rates, and the right product. Bank of Jiangsu's Xiangyingbao, for instance: one-year dollar deposit at 3%, minimum 5,000 dollars.

(2) Always pick a product covered by deposit insurance. It must be a deposit, not a fund.

(3) Keep the annual amount within 50,000 dollars, roughly 330,000 renminbi. Buy 5,000 dollars a month and roll it over.

(4) Leave this money alone. Treat it as the household's long-term reserve, and renew it when it matures. Earn the interest only. If you don't convert it back, you don't need to worry about exchange rate moves, and you don't pay conversion fees.

The whole point is one sentence: simple, safe, and holding its value.
