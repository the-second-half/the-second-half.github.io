Stepped on the scale this morning. The display flickered twice before it settled: 75.0.

Same as yesterday.

Three months ago I would have stood there and sulked. Back then the scale was a judge, handing down a verdict every morning. A drop and I was pleased all day. No movement and the day felt wasted.

I don't think that way anymore.

Ninety-five to seventy-five took 305 days. A little under two kilos a month. Absurdly slow. Search online and you'll find a thousand stories of people losing ten kilos in a month. At my pace, nobody would watch.

But it's real.

Breakfast is still the same three things: 40 grams of oats, two eggs, 250 ml of milk. I've eaten this for nearly a year. Tried variations a few times, always came back. Past fifty, you start to care more about stability — steady meals, steady sleep, even a steady number on the scale feels like good news.

Lunch was in the canteen at work. Half the usual rice, extra vegetables. The woman serving recognises me now and always adds an extra spoonful; I have to say "that's enough, that's enough" before she does.

Around three in the afternoon I got hungry. I used to go downstairs for a pastry. Now I drink a large glass of water and wait it out. Not always successfully — last Wednesday I didn't make it, ate half a packet of biscuits a colleague gave me, cut back on carbs at dinner, and the scale barely moved the next morning.

It took me a long time to understand this: **one slip does not undo everything.** I didn't used to think that. One extra bite and I'd decide the whole day was ruined, then eat freely and start again tomorrow. Not anymore.

At my check-up in June: blood pressure 131/84, fasting glucose 5.8. Better than the start of the year, not yet ideal. The doctor said keep doing what you're doing, no medication needed. I still remember that sentence.

I'm not going lower. At 75 kilos and 175 centimetres, my BMI is 24.5 — the upper edge of normal. That's enough.

What comes next is **holding it**.

A year ago I thought the hard part was losing it. Now I know the hard part is not going back.

Starting tomorrow this journal changes shape. Not "how much did I lose today" but "how did I hold it today". The numbers still get recorded. The mindset is different.

That's it. See you tomorrow.
