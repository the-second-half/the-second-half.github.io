I don't know when I got Hashimoto's. Looking back, it was probably in my thirties — long work pressure, business drinking, late nights, years of being overweight. It was only when a health check measured my thyroid function and some markers came back badly out of range that I learned I had it. I never paid much attention, because Hashimoto's is chronic and gives no obvious symptoms.

## 1. By my mid-forties, three markers were out of range

By my mid-forties, three of the five thyroid markers were out of range and getting worse. The doctor said it had developed into clinical hypothyroidism, that it was affecting my whole metabolism, and that it was also affecting the thyroid nodule. The worst reading was October 2025, when the panel looked like this:

![Figure 1. Thyroid panel and ultrasound, 20 October 2025](/images/thyroid-2025-report-en.png)

## 2. Six rules I set for myself

Hashimoto's is a lifelong immune disease. It cannot be cured, only controlled — protect the thyroid function, and keep it from sliding into hypothyroidism. Protecting and improving my thyroid function, and keeping Hashimoto's under control over the long run, is a lifelong project of my health management. After reading a great deal, I set out these measures for myself:

(1) Hold off on levothyroxine for now, and try diet and lifestyle first.

(2) Eat nothing high in iodine — kelp and seaweed, iodised salt, iodine-rich seafood.

(3) Supplement vitamin D, B vitamins, selenium and zinc. Keep the dose in the middle of the safe range. Selenium, for example, no more than 100 micrograms a day.

(4) Try to get eight hours of sleep every night, and don't stay up late.

(5) Avoid getting emotionally worked up.

(6) Keep warm, and keep the throat from getting chilled and inflamed.

## 3. A year and more later

None of this conflicts with losing weight — each helps the other, and together they are part of my health management. After more than a year of adjustment, and still without levothyroxine, my thyroid function has started to improve and TSH is back in the normal range. The antibodies are still not normal; one of them is about 40 percent below its worst level. The nodule has not grown noticeably.

![Figure 2. Two thyroid panels compared, three ultrasounds for the nodule](/images/thyroid-compare-en.png)

## 4. To close

Hashimoto's thyroiditis is a lifelong disease that cannot be cured. I have had a stage-by-stage success by adjusting how I live. I'll keep going.

Floating, no shore. I am my own boat.
