For most ordinary Chinese between 45 and 65, the picture is the same. Some savings. Earning power falling fast, and it can disappear at any time. Burdens at home and on your own body still heavy. There is a saying here: middle age is a dog's life.

This is also the age when money mistakes cost the most. In a long downturn — like the one running from 2021 to now — traps are everywhere. Even people at the top of business have lost everything.

One book I read in the last two years fits my own thinking almost exactly: *The Psychology of Money*, by Morgan Housel. He is American, a partner at a fund, a columnist for *The Wall Street Journal*. The English original came out in 2020, the Chinese edition in June 2023.

The book makes one point. Doing well with money has little to do with how smart you are, and a lot to do with how you behave. It is not a math problem. It is a psychology problem. Eighteen lessons in all. These are the ones that matter most at our age.

(A table with all the key points is below.)

![Key points from The Psychology of Money](/images/money-psychology-07.png)

1. No one is crazy. What you believe about money comes from the years you happened to live through.

2. Luck and risk are the same coin. Do not take all the credit, and do not take all the blame.

3. "Enough" is the hardest financial skill. The hard part is not earning more. It is knowing when to stop.

4. Compounding runs on time, not on high returns. Of Buffett's 84.5 billion dollars, 81.5 billion came after his 65th birthday. Staying in the game beats winning big.

5. Getting rich takes nerve. Staying rich takes fear. At our age the first part has expired. The second part is the job.

6. Wealth is what you do not see. Money you have not spent is wealth. Saving needs no reason.

7. Leave room for error. Do not fire every bullet at once. The first rule of compounding: never interrupt it without need.

8. Freedom is the highest return. The best thing money buys is control over your own time.

What I actually do:

1. I do not touch what I do not understand.
2. I keep one year of living costs in cash. Not invested, not locked up. It earns the least and lets me sleep.
3. I protect the principal first. After 45, losing half means you need to double just to get back.
4. I lower my expectations. I turn money into certainty: body, mind, cash flow.

In the end this book is not really about money. It is about living with your own wants.

(My own record. Not investment advice.)
