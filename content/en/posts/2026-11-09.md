In February 2026 I put the whole plan into practice. No pilot. No discount. Not one day off.

By the end of October, eight months had passed. My weight went from 95 kilos — the worst it has ever been — to 75. Forty jin.

Most of the markers came back to normal in the same eight months. Blood lipids, liver enzymes, fasting glucose, HbA1c, fatty liver — one by one they moved back into range, and most of the red numbers on the lab report were wiped out. One exception: the thyroid antibodies never budged. Section five says so plainly. I am not going to dress it up.

The fight had a price. Losing weight that fast caused two problems. Constipation led to an anal fistula, and I had surgery on 23 September. And my hair thinned — a patch on one side of the front, handfuls in the sink. Neither is a serious disease. Both are the body filing a complaint. It did not say stop. It said slow down.

Phase one is done. That is the summary.

What follows is all the data: weight, food, calories, lab work, complications, waist. The good and the bad. Because what other people can use is never the conclusion. It is the actions inside the process that can be copied.

## First, the data: what I have and what I do not

Two sources only. Daily notes from DeepSeek, 941 entries. And nineteen lab reports, January 2019 to August 2026.

Three things have to be said up front, or the charts will be misread.

First, the record starts on 25 May 2026, not the beginning of May. The entries for 15–21 May dropped the leading 8 — 85.05 was written as 75.05 — and they do not connect to the 86.35 of 25 May. Corrected by adding ten. The window runs from 25 May.

Second, 299 entries dated 1–29 October in the export are a word-for-word copy of September. The PDF was printed twice. I removed all of them. The real October data sits inside a single entry dated 30 October, covering 23–31 October.

Third, there are no records for 19–29 June, 2–15 July, and 24 September to 22 October. For a missing day I used the average of all real measurements within two weeks either side. The chart shows those stretches as a dotted line, and shades gaps longer than ten days.

Result: 82 mornings with a real fasting weight out of 160 days, and 24 nights with a bedtime weight. Enough for a trend. Not enough for day-by-day precision. Every number below lives inside that limit.

## Weight: two curves, one real fall

Monthly averages of the fasting weight, measured days only:

May (25–31), 6 days: 86.30 kg.
June, 19 days: 84.68, down 1.62.
July, 12 days: 82.04, down 2.64.
August, 18 days: 79.64, down 2.40.
September, 18 days: 77.62, down 2.02.
October (23–31), 9 days: 76.02, down 1.60.

Three things stand out.

The fall was even, not a crash. Between 1.6 and 2.6 kilos a month, with no cliff and no plateau longer than three weeks. That pace is the kindest to muscle and metabolism.

Bedtime weight sat a steady 1.5 to 2.5 kilos above the fasting weight, and the two curves ran almost parallel. So the daily noise is water and gut contents, not fat coming and going.

The surgery on 23 September was the only break in the line. A week after it my weight bounced to 78.55 — fluids, bed rest, nothing moving — and by the end of October it was 74.80, which is 1.05 kilos below the pre-surgery low of 75.85.

## Food: five things carried every day

Counting rules: a food mentioned three times in one day counts once. Only foods appearing on three days or more are listed. Coverage is 53 per cent — 68 logged days out of 129 — so the monthly figure is the share of logged days multiplied by 30.

The five staples, all above ten times a month: eggs (78 per cent of logged days, 23.4 a month, one or two boiled at a meal); leafy greens (59 per cent, 17.6, 200 grams); skimmed milk (53 per cent, 15.9, 200 ml); oats (46 per cent, 13.7, 80 grams); firm tofu (37 per cent, 11.0, 250–300 grams).

Another twenty-one items show up occasionally, under ten times a month: salmon and kabocha squash at 9.3 each, potatoes 7.9, carrots and almonds 7.1, cherry tomatoes and pumpkin seeds 6.6, chicken breast 5.7, tomatoes 5.3, prunes 4.9, taro, button mushrooms and apples 4.4, tofu skin, king oyster mushrooms and okra 4.0, beef, mixed-grain buns and bananas 3.1, shiitake 2.2, congee 1.3.

Supplements are four plus one. A multivitamin, one a day, half an hour after breakfast. Vitamin D, 400 IU, same time. Selenium yeast, raised from 50 to 100 micrograms in August. Zinc gluconate, 20 ml twice a day, mid-morning and an hour after dinner, kept two hours away from milk and oats. Magnesium glycinate, 200 mg as needed at bedtime, four hours away from levothyroxine.

The whole diet in one line: a fixed breakfast of eggs, greens, oats and milk; lunch and dinner built on boiled protein, a lot of greens, and mushrooms; red meat once or twice a week; salmon every other day; fruit limited, nuts counted by the piece. The only oils are olive and perilla, and most meals use none.

## Calories: the gap was wider than I thought

![Daily intake against maintenance, and the gap between them](/images/health-2026-gap.png)

On the 48 days where at least two meals were logged: median intake 1,136 kcal, mean 1,322, range 523 to 2,879. Median maintenance 2,156 kcal, from Mifflin-St Jeor times an activity factor of 1.30. Median gap 1,026 kcal a day, mean 835. Cumulative gap about 40,087 kcal, roughly 5.2 kilos of fat.

Weight tells a different story: 10.5 kilos over 129 days works out to about 630 kcal a day.

The two figures differ by around 30 per cent, and the reason is simple. The model only counts food that was written down. Meals out, snacks and cooking oil were not recorded, so intake is underestimated and the gap is exaggerated. Read the chart this way: the direction and the scale are right, the absolute number is too big.

What does deserve attention is the median gap of 1,026 kcal — nearly double the usual 500 to 600 kcal recommendation. Fine for a while. Expensive over time. Section six is the bill.

## Lab work: lipids reversed, thyroid loosened one notch

Only markers that were abnormal in 2023 or 2024 are listed, even if they later recovered. Bold in the tables means outside the reference range.

![Abnormal markers across four rounds of testing](/images/health-2026-labs.png)

![Imaging findings, then and now](/images/health-2026-imaging.png)

Four sentences cover it.

Lipids are the big win. Triglycerides went 4.56 in October 2025, to 2.32, to 0.83 — inside the reference range. Total cholesterol 5.75 to 6.72 to 5.19. LDL 3.12 to 3.96 to 2.94. All three improved together. That is the most direct return on losing weight.

The thyroid loosened one notch. TSH fell from a peak of 10.74 to 7.24 to 4.96. Still above the 4.2 ceiling, but the lowest in two years. The antibodies did not move: thyroglobulin antibodies stayed above 4,000, peroxidase antibodies sat between 179 and 189. The autoimmune attack did not stop because I lost weight.

The liver cleaned up. ALT and AST came down from 109.88 and 60.08 in December 2019 and settled around 20. GGT fell from 103 to 15.3, the largest drop of any marker.

The gallbladder is the one regression. On 18 June 2026 the scan showed cholecystitis with a wall 4.3 mm thick, the thickest in years. A very low-fat diet plus fast weight loss leaves bile sitting and the gallbladder emptying less. That is a known cost, not an accident.

## Complications: a rough half year

![Complications and abnormalities during the period](/images/health-2026-complication.png)

Eight items. A lump near the anus on 28 August, which was the fistula, operated on 23 September; surgery finished at 1:30 in the afternoon, and urine did not come until 10:30 that night. Constipation from 20 May, logged on ten days, with a note on 31 July that it had clearly eased, and a return in October. Abnormal stool from 19 May, twelve days, dry and dark, with one entry on 23 August reading "very smelly, very dark". Hair loss from 15 August, worse by 31 August, a strip about a third of a palm wide on one side of the front, scalp visible. Loose skin from 11 September, jawline sharper, belly thinner, skin on the hips slack. One episode of blood in stool on 21 May, a trace on the gauze. One episode of diarrhoea on 1 September.

Hair loss and constipation belong together. Both appeared in the fastest phase, mid-August to early September, when the gap was widest and protein and fibre were easiest to run short of. The pattern of the hair loss — patchy, strip-shaped, fast — fits telogen effluvium after rapid weight loss. It usually recovers three to six months after weight stabilises. The condition is that the deficit stops growing.

## Three answers

Weight. High of 86.55 on 28 May, low of 74.80 on 30 October. Down 11.75 kilos. Monthly average from 86.30 to 76.02, down 10.28. Against the all-time peak of 95.5, the low is 20.7 kilos lower, 21.7 per cent.

Waist. Low of 82.0 centimetres on 30 October, 18.0 below the peak of 100. Reliable data starts at 84.5 on 15 August, then another 2.5 centimetres in two and a half months. The waist moved slower than the weight, which says the early loss was mostly visceral fat and water.

Markers. Twelve of them, in the table.

![Twelve key markers, then and now](/images/health-2026-metrics.png)

Put the three lines together and this is what they say.

Weight moved first, markers moved second, antibodies never moved at all. Weight started falling clearly in June. Lipids only turned in June and were fully normal by September. TSH only dropped below 5 in June. The thyroid antibodies did not change from beginning to end. Losing weight works directly on metabolic markers — lipids, glucose, liver enzymes. It does almost nothing to an autoimmune process. Hashimoto's is managed by monitoring and medication, not by diet.

The price is real too. A gap of about 1,000 kcal a day for months bought me hair loss, constipation and cholecystitis. None of the three is a disease. All three say the same thing: slower would have been better.

## Method and limits

Weight: fasting readings come from "this morning", "this morning early" or "on waking" plus "weight"; bedtime readings from "before bed" or "tonight" plus "weight". Multiple readings on one day use the median.

Interpolation: a missing day takes the average of all real measurements within two weeks either side. Gaps longer than ten days are shaded.

Food frequency: counted by days, once per day. Monthly figure equals share of logged days times 30, correcting for 53 per cent coverage.

Calories: values from the sixth edition of the Chinese Food Composition Tables, edible portion. Quantities from the text where given, typical portions otherwise. Baseline is Mifflin-St Jeor times 1.30, at 178 cm and 50 years old.

Lab work: only markers abnormal in 2023 or 2024. Blood pressure exists only for January 2019; later reports did not keep the general examination page, so no value is given. I have not measured it since, and that is a debt I owe myself.

This is a personal record, not medical advice. Any change to medication or supplements belongs with a doctor.
