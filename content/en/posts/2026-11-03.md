A decision you make because you are afraid of dying is not the same as a decision you reach because you have woken up.

I knew what had to be done. Eat less, move more, and the numbers would turn. Accept reality, adjust my thinking, and the anxiety would ease. Keep working, even with income cut off, and hold on to the job. Knowing it was the easy part.

And then I made the same mistake I had always made: I wanted all of it at once. I set a goal, then gave myself the shortest possible time to reach the best possible result. Lose 40 jin, twenty kilos, back to normal weight. Lose no muscle. No hair falling out, no bald spot. Skin that does not sag. Work undisturbed. The necessary business dinners handled. A mood that stays sunny and positive. The money I had put out coming back. I wrote a long list of goals, every one of them the best possible outcome, and all of it to be done within a year.

I believed that if I wanted it badly enough, and held on hard enough, I would get there.

I did change a great deal. (1) Fewer meals out, fewer of the snacks I like, dried hawthorn slices for instance. (2) Less drinking. When I was low I used to buy braised meat and cold dishes and drink beer or rice wine alone. Now I mostly do not drink at all, or drink a dry yellow wine, low in alcohol. (3) I started steaming vegetables in the office. Even the dumplings I like, I have them with vegetable filling. (4) I walked whenever there was time, aiming at five to ten kilometres a day. (5) Dinner as little as possible, sometimes none; at weekends maybe one day of near-fasting. (6) Reading, scrolling, looking for anything that would settle the mind — the old philosophers, Zen, the Diamond Sutra, Liaofan's Four Lessons, self-help books from abroad. Trying to get calm from outside, to find some release. And more of the same.

From December 2023 to December 2024 I kept at it for about a year. Some things did move. My weight went from 95 kilos to around 88. The fatty liver was better than it had been at its worst. Blood sugar came down. I managed to hold the basic income steady and cut the large unnecessary expenses, and the tightening kept the household going. But that was all. Set against what I had aimed for, it was far short. The health warning had not been lifted.

Worse: because I wanted it so badly, I made myself more anxious. Controlling what I ate meant a daily fight between willpower and appetite, and that fight felt awful. There were signs I could not keep it up.

So where was the problem?
